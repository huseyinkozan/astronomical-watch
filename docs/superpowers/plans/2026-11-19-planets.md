# Planets Page Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A planets page (solar system, sky, rise and set, distance) between the information and
settings pages, computed on the watch and host-tested against Skyfield.

**Architecture:** `main/Planets.*` (plain C++) computes heliocentric and geocentric positions
from JPL's approximate elements, altitude and azimuth through `Astronomy::starPosition`, and the
day's rise and set by the Sun's crossing search. `main/PlanetPages.*` draws the four parts in a
`StackPage`: two drawn objects and two `TablePart`s. The day's rise and set times are computed in
the main task on the page's request (as the clock page's Hijri request), positions once a minute.

**Tech Stack:** ESP-IDF v6.1, LVGL 9, C++17; Skyfield with DE421 for the reference values.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-19-planets-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- Page order: watch face · clock · information · **planets** · settings.
- Parts: 1 solar system (square-root radii, Saturn 180 px; anticlockwise, vernal equinox to the
  right; no text), 2 sky (circle R 160, horizon through the centre, "E"/"W" at its ends, lower half
  dotted and dim, Sun and Moon drawn), 3 rise and set table, 4 distance table (AU two decimals,
  light time in minutes).
- Colours: Mercury 0xAAAAAA, Venus 0xF0E1AA, Earth 0x5A8CFF, Mars 0xE65A3C, Jupiter 0xE1BE8C,
  Saturn 0xDCBE6E; names in the tables in their colours.
- Accuracy: Mercury–Mars within about 1′; Jupiter and Saturn about 2′ after the great-inequality
  terms if Table 1 alone is above 2′; rise and set within 2 min at mid latitudes, a stated wider
  tolerance at Tromsø.
- Every text in `main/Language.*` in English and Turkish; CLAUDE.md style.
- The page holds no heap far from the shown page or while the portal runs unless shown.
- No change to the minute wake or the faces.

## Review Focus

1. A planet that does not rise or set on the local day (circumpolar or below the horizon all day
   at Tromsø, Reykjavik): "--:--", never a wrong time.
2. The local day boundary: a set just after local midnight belongs to the next day; the request
   is made again when the local day changes while the page is shown.
3. The page opened before the rise and set times are computed: the table shows "--:--" and fills
   in, without a freeze of the swipe.
4. Southern hemisphere (Sydney): the sky part's east and west stay left and right (the view is
   from the side facing south everywhere); planets culminating north appear correctly mirrored.
5. Portal order and hidden parts of the planets page, including all hidden (one part stays), and a
   page freed and built again on a hidden part (`StackPage::restore`).
