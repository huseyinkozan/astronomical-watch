Tools

Torch

The whole screen in one colour: red that keeps your night vision, amber, white, or SOS flashes (with beeps or without).

  • Hold, then drag sideways: colour (red, amber, white, SOS, SOS with beeps, red again; dragged left, the other way round). Up or down: brightness.
  • It switches off after 2 minutes and the watch sleeps; every tap starts the time again.

Calculator

A scientific calculator; the result shows in grey while you type, large after =.

  • fn shows the functions (sin, √, log...), then their inverses. DEG/RAD changes the angle unit.
  • C clears; ⌫ takes back the last key. Brackets close by themselves at the end.

Unit converter

Angle and time (h:m:s, degrees, radians), distance (km, AU, light years, parsecs), temperature (°C, °F, K).

  • Tap the title: the kind. Tap a value: enter it in that unit with the keypad; = takes it.

Spirit level

Lay the watch on a surface: a bubble and the two tilts show. 30-45° (avalanche slopes) shows in amber.

  • Tap the title: sighting. Look along the top edge at a top: the height from a distance, or the distance from a height.
  • A tap on the angle holds it; a tap on the distance lets you enter it.

Counter

For dhikr, laps or any tally; the rate per hour shows too.

  • +1 counts, the arrow takes one back. The target key: none, 33, 99, 100 or three times 33.
  • Holding the number resets it. The BOOT key counts too when set so.

Random

  • Dice, a coin or a number from 1 to 100; a tap rolls again. The dice key again changes the number of dice (1-3).

Metronome

30 to 240 beats a minute at the alarm's lowest level; the CPR key sets 110 for chest compressions. Show it first under Pages.

  • - and + step by one; held, by five. Tap the dots: the bar (none, 2, 3, 4; the first beat higher).
  • While it runs the screen stays on; it stops 10 minutes after the last tap, or when you leave the part.

Emergency card

Name, blood type, allergies, medication, a contact and phone, a note: entered in this page's Settings tab. It shows on Tools while a field is filled.

Observing

Polar alignment

Where Polaris (sigma Octantis in the south) goes on a polar scope's clock reticle, its clock reading and hour angle.

  • Shown inverted as most polar scopes do; a tap turns it upright.

Milky Way window

Tonight's hours of full darkness with the galactic centre 10° up and the Moon out of the way, the centre's highest, the Moon, and the next night with a window.

Deep-sky finder

The Messier objects 10° up now, highest first: altitude and tonight's best time.

  • The list scrolls; a swipe past its end steps the part.

Eyepieces

With the telescope and eyepieces of the settings: magnification, exit pupil, true field, limiting magnitude and resolution.

  • A tap steps to the next eyepiece.

Night photo

The longest exposure without star trails (500 and NPF rules), the exposure behind an ND filter and a star-trail series' frames; with the lens and sensor of the settings.

  • Tap the title: the calculator.

Nautical almanac

For a sextant fix: the GHA and declination of the Sun, the Moon or a planet at the UTC second, semi-diameter, parallax, noon or meridian passage. Show it first under Pages.

  • A tap steps to the next body.

Astrolabe

An astrolabe for your place. Tap the title: the dial, the alidade on the Sun and on Polaris, a sundial, the analemma.

  • On the dial a sideways drag turns the time; a tap returns to now.
  • Alidade: point the top edge at the Sun (by its shadow) or at Polaris; the time or the latitude follows. A tap holds the angle.

Aiming

A satellite dish's elevation and azimuth, the magnetic variation (WMM2025), the compass bearing and when the Sun stands in that direction; or a solar panel's tilt. Show it first under Pages.

  • Tap the title: dish or panel. Tap the satellite: the next one (your own longitude is in the settings).
